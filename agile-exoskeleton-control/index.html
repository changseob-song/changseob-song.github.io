Methods

Project overview

We explore agile lower-limb exoskeleton control through human motion priors and musculoskeletal simulation. An adversarial motion prior learned from a small set of reference motion clips supports diverse locomotor behaviors across uneven terrain. The project brings together learning from human movement and physically grounded policy training.

Methods

We first train a muscle-driven human model to move across varied terrain, then jointly learn human movement and hip-exoskeleton assistance.

Curriculum and training

Proximal policy optimization (PPO) trains the policy in 4,096 parallel GAIT simulations at 40 Hz. Body state and a terrain height map inform the actions, while adaptive sampling gives difficult terrain conditions more training experience. The demonstrations shown here use training terrain layouts.

Reward formulation

r = wtask rtask + wAMP rAMP

The task reward balances commanded-speed tracking, muscle metabolic effort, foot placement, and exoskeleton effort. Riser impacts and unstable edge contacts receive penalties. As the terrain expands, training places greater emphasis on the task reward while retaining the motion prior.

AMP motion prior

The adversarial motion prior (AMP) compares simulated motion transitions with reference clips of level-ground movement, slopes, and stairs to provide a learned motion-style reward. This guides the policy toward human-like movement while allowing task-driven adaptation. Uneven-ground motion is learned through simulation without uneven-ground demonstrations.

During terrain adaptation, discriminator updates use only conditions covered by the demonstrations. During exoskeleton training, the discriminator is frozen so the human model can adapt to assistance under a consistent motion prior.

Exoskeleton control

For each hip, the policy selects flexion and extension equilibrium angles and an assistance-mode command. An equilibrium-angle controller blends flexion assistance, extension assistance, and transparent behavior, then converts these commands into hip torque.

Flexion and extension targets are centered at 45° and −30°, respectively, to support assistance through the intended movement phase. Commands are smoothed, and torque is limited to 22 N·m per hip. Human muscle commands and exoskeleton commands are jointly optimized using metabolic effort and torque costs, allowing the device to remain nearly transparent when assistance is unnecessary.
