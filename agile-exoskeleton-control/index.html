Project overview

Human motion priors meet physics-based locomotion learning.

We explore agile lower-limb exoskeleton control through human motion priors and musculoskeletal simulation. An adversarial motion prior learned from a small set of reference motion clips supports diverse locomotor behaviors across uneven terrain. The project brings together learning from human movement and physically grounded policy training.

1. Curriculum and training mechanics

Training progresses from conditions covered by, or between, reference demonstrations to a broader simulated terrain set. Hip assistance is enabled only after this human locomotion policy has been established.

Simulation and policy
GAIT GPU simulation with the H1018 2-D musculoskeletal model. The policy has 228 observations, including a terrain height map, and 25 action channels: 18 muscle excitations, six hip-exoskeleton commands, and one lumbar command.
Model configuration
All stages use added midfoot arch contact spheres and a 1.3× multiplier on maximum isometric muscle force. The metabolic model’s muscle mass scales with this force change.
PPO updates
Each iteration collects 40 control steps per world. PPO uses two epochs with 40 minibatches of 4,096 transitions; generalized advantage estimation uses γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95. Episodes last at most 1,000 control steps (25 s).
Adaptive terrain sampling
All levels within a stage are available from its start. Levels are sampled in proportion to difficulty scores bounded between 0.1 and 1. A fall raises the score by 0.1; a successful timeout lowers it by 0.05, allocating more experience to difficult conditions.
Stable stage transitions
Observation normalization is frozen after the first five million frames of Stage A. Checkpoints preserve curriculum scores for shared levels; newly introduced levels start at score 1.
Resets and episode handling
Stair episodes start on a flat landing. Low torso height, excessive ground reaction force (>3 body weights after an initial grace period), or numerical divergence ends an episode. In Stage C, a timeout succeeds if distance traveled is at least min(10 m, 0.6 × commanded speed × 25 s).
Evaluation and checkpoints
Periodic evaluation runs every 50 iterations over 32 worlds. Training checkpoints are saved every two iterations. The current evaluation and video exports use the trained terrain set.

2. Reward formulation

At each control step, an additive task objective is combined with the AMP motion-style reward. The stage weights are given in the curriculum table.

r = wtask rtask + wAMP rAMP

rtask = rvel − 2.5 × 10−4 Pmet − 0.5 Ckick − 1.0 Cedge − 0.05 Cexo

Terms inside the task reward
TermDefinition and purpose
rvel · Speed trackingThe speed component is 1 inside a ±0.1 m/s band around the command and decays as exp(−2.04 d²) beyond it, where d is the error beyond that band. It is multiplied by exp(−0.1 ωtorso²) to discourage torso oscillation.
Pmet · Muscle effortGross metabolic power of the 18 muscles, in watts, computed with an Umberger/Uchida-based metabolic model.
Ckick · Riser contactCounts feet with a heel/toe contact dominated by horizontal force, |Fx| > |Fy|, identifying impacts against a riser face.
Cedge · Unsafe edge placementDetects loaded arch contacts or geometric edge placement: a loaded foot on a corner, perched near a drop, or straddling a riser. Geometry checks require a foot load of at least 0.25 body weights.
Cexo · Assistance costSum over both hips of [τexo / (22 N·m)]². This term is zero while the exoskeleton is off.

Riser and edge penalties apply on stairs and uneven ground after a 26-step reset grace period. Edge contacts incur penalties rather than immediate termination, preserving opportunities to learn recovery and foot placement. There is no alive bonus or explicit fall penalty; a fall ends the remaining reward stream.

3. The AMP prior’s role

Reference clips contain level-ground motion, slopes at ±5° and ±10°, and stair ascent/descent. There are no uneven-ground demonstrations. An adversarial motion prior (AMP) supplies a learned style reward, guiding the PPO policy toward human-like motion while the task objective drives speed tracking, terrain negotiation, and effort reduction.

rAMP = max[0, 1 − ¼ (D(φt−1, φt) − 1)²]

The least-squares GAN discriminator scores two consecutive normalized 24-dimensional motion features. They describe pelvis and joint kinematics, feet relative to the pelvis, and torso direction. The discriminator receives neither terrain geometry nor commanded speed.

Keep the prior relevant

In Stage B, discriminator updates use only demonstration-covered worlds. This avoids teaching it to reject terrain-adaptive movements simply because uneven-ground examples are absent from the demonstrations. The style reward is still applied everywhere, with its weight reduced to 0.3.

Allow assisted movement to adapt

In Stage C, the discriminator is frozen as a fixed unassisted-motion prior while human muscle commands and exoskeleton commands are jointly optimized. This prevents the discriminator from continually redefining assistance-induced motion changes as undesirable. Kinematic drift remains a check for ongoing evaluation.

4. Exoskeleton controller design

Each hip uses an equilibrium-angle controller. The policy outputs a flexion equilibrium angle, an extension equilibrium angle, and a gait-mode command. These two three-command groups form six exoskeleton action channels. The controller blends flexion assistance, extension assistance, and transparent behavior, converting the commands into hip torque at each control step.
